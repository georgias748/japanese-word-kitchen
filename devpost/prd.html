Japanese Word Kitchen

A cozy Japanese restaurant-inspired vocabulary app where each learned word helps cook a recognizable dish.

Product approved. The agreed behaviors and proof-of-concept boundary guide the build.

The complete journey

From the restaurant doorway to a meal served across the counter—and back home, ready to study again.

Restaurant home, words, and review

The home is a small, cozy Japanese restaurant seen from its doorway, with a busy grandparent couple, counter, personal black-and-white photographs, a bonsai, and carefully chosen cultural details. Food-themed navigation offers Let's Study, vocabulary management, and Review.

Custom English-Japanese pairs can be added, edited, and deleted. Both sides are required. A duplicate pair is ignored with a friendly, in-world message. The 12 built-in pairs make the first session possible without custom words.

Review presents missed words one at a time with the same flip-and-rate interaction. Ratings 3–4 remove a word from the tray; ratings 1–2 keep it. Review does not add ingredients.

Persistence: Custom words and missed words remain on the same device/browser between visits. No sign-in, syncing, or sharing.

What success looks like

  • Home offers study and vocabulary management, and Review when the tray is non-empty.
  • Blank pairs cannot be saved; duplicates leave one copy and display a styled notice.
  • Custom words and missed words remain available after returning on the same device/browser.
Study setup and the 15-dish menu

Before study, choose English-front/Japanese-answer or Japanese-front/English-answer. Each session randomly selects three different dishes. Each dish's ingredient count also sets its card count. Sample distinct custom words first, then fill any remaining slots from the 12 built-in pairs. A word pair never repeats in one session.

Study table, ingredients, and dish reveal

At the very top of each study view, show only the dish name in Japanese, a dash, and its English name. The Japanese spelling will be prepared with the dish content. Study takes place at a quiet tabletop, with the vocabulary word in the middle and a Flip button at the bottom right. Pressing it reveals the other language and makes the 1–4 ratings appear at the same time:

3–4 · Earned Add the next predefined, authentic ingredient to the dish.

1–2 · Missed Add no ingredient; put the word in the tray for later. Do not repeat it in this session.

As words are earned, their ingredient pictures appear individually side by side on the plate in the restaurant's cartoon style. After the dish's three or four cards are complete, a little star-pop presents the whole assembled dish. Each dish has a predetermined final illustration for every possible number of earned ingredients: four variants for a three-ingredient dish (0–3 earned), or five for a four-ingredient dish (0–4 earned). Unearned ingredients are absent, and fewer earned words make the same dish look less colorful and blander. Then show a short cultural fact on decorative paper.

Visual review: Open the complete dish illustration gallery to compare all 15 dishes at every earned-ingredient count.

After flipping a study or review card, the learner can press Hear Japanese to hear the Japanese word spoken by an installed Japanese browser/device voice. It works for built-in and custom words in either card direction. If speech synthesis or a Japanese voice is unavailable, explain the requirement instead of failing silently. This uses speech synthesis rather than recorded clips or an external audio service.

Aim for three short cultural facts per dish, with two as the minimum. Facts for a dish do not repeat until that dish's fact set is exhausted.

What success looks like

  • The dish name is at the very top in Japanese, then a dash and English; the word is centered, and **Flip** is bottom right.
  • Pressing **Flip** reveals the answer and displays all four ratings at the same time.
  • After flipping a study or review card, **Hear Japanese** can speak built-in and custom words when a Japanese voice is installed.
  • A rating of 3 or 4 visibly adds one ingredient beside the others; 1 or 2 does not.
  • After the last card, a star-pop presents the predetermined assembled-dish illustration for the number of ingredients earned.
  • Three-ingredient dishes have four final appearance variants; four-ingredient dishes have five.
  • Ingredients belong to that dish; a fact appears after complete and unfinished dishes alike.
  • A dish's facts cycle without repetition until each available fact has appeared.
Serve the meal and meet the customers

After the third dish and its fact, a brief “Your food has been served!” message leads into a view from the chef's side of the counter. Three Japanese aunties react to the meal:

  • Happy: all three dishes are complete.
  • Neutral: exactly one dish is unfinished.
  • Upset: two or three dishes are unfinished.

After their reaction, the app returns to restaurant home, where the learner can study again, manage custom words, or review.

What success looks like

  • The served message and customer view follow the third dish.
  • Their expression matches the number of unfinished dishes.
  • The flow returns to the restaurant home.
Look, feel, and what we are not building

The visual direction draws on Heian-period art, warm yellow papyrus-like paper, and an ink-brush feeling in decorative notes and brief messages, interpreted originally rather than copying a specific artist or artwork. Keep study text readable. The restaurant is detailed but not cluttered; the tabletop is calmer. Ingredient art and feedback notes belong to the same charming cartoon world.

In the proof of concept: one home, custom vocabulary, review, a 15-dish menu, three random dishes per session, 12 built-in pairs, dish-specific facts and ingredients, the study flow, Japanese word pronunciation through an installed browser/device voice, and the counter ending.

Deferred: accounts, cross-device sync, sharing, advanced spaced repetition, analytics, a larger menu, custom dishes, other cuisines, restaurant simulation, background music, cooking effects, and recorded studio audio.

Content prep for the next phase: define the exact 12 built-in pairs, two or more accurate facts for each dish, and each dish's three or four authentic key ingredients.
