# Kitchen of Words

A small web app that turns Japanese vocabulary practice into a cozy recipe-building experience, where each word becomes an ingredient or step in preparing a traditional dish.

## The Unique Kernel
The distinctive thing is that learning is not just memorizing words in isolation: every vocabulary item becomes part of building a real dish. The app feels like a tiny Japanese kitchen experience, where progress is visible as a meal comes together one ingredient at a time. That gives the language practice a personal, creative identity instead of feeling like another plain flashcard tool.

## Who It's For
A beginner language learner who is passionate about Japanese culture, food, and beautiful, thoughtful design. They want a study habit that feels calming and motivating, not like a generic productivity tool. They are learning Japanese for personal meaning and want a study experience that fits their interests.

## The Core Loop
The user opens the app, chooses a dish or meal, studies a small set of Japanese words related to it, and marks what they know. Each learned word adds an ingredient, cooking step, or part of the final dish. The user keeps returning to complete a few dishes, each one representing a small but satisfying milestone in their language progress.

## Inspiration & Identity
Inspired by the warm, intimate feel of cooking and a calm Japanese aesthetic. The mood should be cozy, elegant, and rewarding rather than overly gamified. It should feel like a miniature culinary world where each lesson makes the dish more complete.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
They want a project that feels meaningful and personal, not just efficient. A kitchen-themed learning app reflects their interest in Japanese culture and food while keeping the project approachable for a beginner hackathon. It also makes vocabulary practice feel tied to something they can imagine using in real life.

## What "Working" Looks Like
The proof of concept is a simple app where the learner selects a dish, reviews a small set of Japanese vocabulary related to it, and sees the dish gradually build as words are learned. The “oh, that’s cool” moment is when a few study actions visibly transform a collection of ingredients into a finished dish, making the language learning feel concrete and rewarding.

## The POC Boundary
In scope:
- a small set of dishes or meal categories
- a small number of Japanese words connected to each dish
- learning and marking words as known or reviewed
- visual progress as ingredients or steps are added to a dish
- a short, demoable core loop that can be shown in under a minute

Out of scope for now:
- full restaurant management or simulation
- broad menu systems or many cuisines
- advanced spaced repetition logic
- multiplayer features or user accounts
- backend storage, authentication, or large content libraries

## Later
- more dishes and more vocabulary sets
- richer animations and polish
- difficulty levels or custom dish creation
- sound effects, the ability to “serve” the meal, or recipe cards
- saved progress and repetition tracking

## Explicitly Cut
- A full restaurant app with ordering, customer flows, or a business sim: too much for a beginner hackathon and not necessary for the core idea.
- A general Japanese vocab app with no distinctive identity: this would be too generic and lose the creative hook.
- A huge content library or advanced learning system: not needed to prove the concept.
